# MAYOR OF CAT CITY
Game Design Document
Session: August 2026  |  Status: Early Concept / Ideation

## 1. Tone & Themes

Mayor of Cat City is a happy, earnest game with cat puns throughout and a genuine heart underneath. Nobody stays a villain. Every enemy, including Big Dog, gets a chance to be understood and redeemed. The capture mechanic reflects this — you are de-escalating, not defeating.

- Tone: Light, comedic, warm — cat puns are the vehicle, kindness is the destination
- Theme: Redemption — conflict stems from unresolved hurt, resolution comes from acknowledgment
- Ending: Happy. Big Dog is converted, not defeated. The gang follows.
- Violence level: Nobody gets hurt. Dizzy and captured is as far as it goes.

This tone should inform all writing decisions — dialogue, level naming, character design, puns. Keep it earnest. The joke is never mean.

## 2. Overview

Mayor of Cat City is a 2D side-scrolling platformer set in a city of cats. The player is appointed interim mayor of Cat City, which is in disrepair and partially occupied by Big Dog and his gang. By fighting through zones across the city, the player earns the right to become permanent mayor.

- Genre: 2D side-scrolling platformer (current build scope)
- Protagonist: The Mayor — a cat, appointed interim mayor
- Primary villain: Big Dog — redeemable, motivated by a perceived childhood slight
- Core pun origin: Police cat puns (e.g. Pawcifer Jones)
- Engine: Godot (recommended — excellent 2D tooling, free, open source)

Note: The larger vision (mini-game collection, trading sim, Animal Kingdom) is documented in Section 9 (Future Scope) and should inform structural decisions but is not part of the current build target.

## 3. Big Dog — Villain & Backstory

### 3.1 Character

Big Dog is the primary antagonist of Cat City. He runs a gang that has taken over parts of the city, orchestrated heists, and generally caused chaos. He is not evil — he is a dog who never got over something that happened when he was a puppy.

### 3.2 Motivation

Big Dog's entire criminal enterprise is downstream of a perceived slight from his puppyhood. The slight involved cats — probably something minor that he catastrophised, or something genuinely small that nobody else remembers. He has been the architect of his own misery ever since.

- The pettier the slight, the better — funnier and more poignant
- Specific nature of the slight: TBD — worth deciding as it informs the ending
- He is not sympathetic enough to undercut the comedy, but human enough to deserve resolution

### 3.3 Arc

Big Dog escapes at the end of each level until the final confrontation. When the slight is finally acknowledged — however that plays out — he deflates. The whole criminal empire was holding up one unresolved hurt. Once that's gone, so is the reason to fight.

- Ending: Big Dog is converted, becomes an ally or friend
- The final resolution should acknowledge the original slight somehow — silly and a little sweet

## 4. Combat & Core Mechanics

### 4.1 Basic Attack — Scratch

- Melee scratch attack
- Primary tool for all combat

### 4.2 Power Bar

- Fills by landing scratch hits on enemies
- Also fills from pickups scattered through levels
- Can be spent at any charge level as an AOE roar attack
- Scales in power and radius as charge level increases

Charge levels:
- Level 1: Moderate AOE, audible roar
- Level 2: Stronger AOE, louder roar
- Level 3: Stronger still, louder still
- Max charge: Large AOE, kitten yell — knocks mini-bosses dizzy instantly

The kitten yell at max charge is intentional — classic bait-and-switch on player expectations. The roar escalates menacingly and then peaks at a tiny squeak. It is also the most powerful attack in the game.

### 4.3 Enemy Behaviour — Regular Gang Members

- Palette swaps of a single enemy type for now
- Seek the player when they enter sight range
- Leash back to their area when the player leaves range
- Melee attack only
- Enough scratch hits to dizzy them — dizzy = captured, no further mechanic needed for now

### 4.4 Enemy Behaviour — Mini-Bosses

- Same as regular enemies, more hits to dizzy
- Max charge roar knocks them dizzy instantly
- Each mini-boss guards a sub-area of their level

## 5. Levels

### Level 1 — The Pawlice Station

#### Premise

Big Dog's gang has taken over the Cat City Pawlice Station. The Mayor must fight through to capture the gang and confront Big Dog. Big Dog escapes at the end.

#### Layout

- Main hallway is the spine of the level
- Doors off the hallway lead to sub-areas — each contains a mini-boss
- Defeating all mini-bosses unlocks access to Big Dog
- Natural room types: lobby/front desk, bullpen, holding cells, evidence room, rooftop
- Holding cells could be where captured citizens are held — optional narrative beat

#### Enemies

- Regular gang members throughout — palette swaps, same behaviour
- Several mini-boss gang members, one per sub-area
- Final encounter: Big Dog — specific mechanics TBD, he escapes after

#### Visuals

- Environment: Police station interior — desks, filing cabinets, bulletin boards, holding cell bars
- Platforms: desks, cabinets, counters, stairs

### Level 2 — The Museum

#### Premise

Big Dog escaped and is now attempting to heist something from the Cat City Museum. The Mayor must move through the museum without being seen, outsmart the guards, and stop the heist. Big Dog is foiled by a puzzle rather than a fight.

#### Core Mechanic — Light and Shadow

- Light and dark areas determine visibility
- Stay in dark — guards cannot see you
- Step into light — exposed, guard is alerted
- Light sources can be manipulated: switches, thrown objects to break bulbs

#### Guards

- Vision cone — alerted if player is in light and within cone
- In dark: invisible to guards regardless of position
- Distraction mechanic: throw something to make noise, guard investigates
- Hitman-style noise lure — move a guard off their path without confrontation

#### Final Encounter

- Logic puzzle — open a chest, get a key, open a door, stop the heist
- Silly and narratively satisfying — Big Dog gets foiled by a puzzle
- No direct combat with Big Dog here
- Big Dog escapes again

#### Open Questions

- Does getting seen result in instant fail/reset, or trigger a chase?
- Can the roar be used here, or does noise work against you?
- What did Big Dog come to steal? TBD — worth deciding for narrative payoff

### Level 3 — The Cat City Renaissance Faire

#### Premise

Big Dog has fled to the Cat City Renaissance Faire (working title — needs a pun). The Mayor must hunt him through the crowds and chaos of the faire. Big Dog may be running the faire as a front, or simply hiding in it.

#### Style — Assassin's Creed 2D

- Movement-first gameplay — free running, climbing, traversal as the primary skill
- Dense vertical environment: tent poles, rooftops, banners, towers, market stalls
- Blend into crowds to lose pursuers or avoid detection
- Stealth captures from above or from cover
- Combat exists but is secondary to movement and positioning

#### Environment

- Medieval faire setting: jousting areas, food stalls, stages, jesters, banners
- Lots of vertical surfaces to climb and leap between
- Dense crowds of faire-goers usable as cover

#### Final Encounter

- TBD — chase/hunt framing suits the setting better than a static boss fight
- Does Big Dog escape again, or is this a turning point?

## 9. Future Scope

Out of scope for current build but should inform structural decisions.

### 9.1 Additional Levels (Candidate Zones)

- The Haunted Gardens — atmospheric, ghost enemies, maze-like
- The Foundries — industrial, machinery hazards
- Cat City Power Works — electrical, vertical, high voltage
- City Hall — bureaucratic maze, paper-pusher enemies
- The Market District — chaotic, possible chase sequence
- The Sewers — underground, rat enemies
- The Docks — waterfront, Big Dog importing something, lieutenant here
- The Rooftops — pure vertical traversal, wind mechanics
- The Train Yard — moving platforms, trains as hazard
- The Junkyard — Big Dog's home territory, mechanical enemies
- The Theatre — stage machinery as platforms, spotlight hazards

### 9.2 Mini-Game Collection Layer

- Gardens becomes top-down action RPG (Secret of Evermore style)
- Rope-swinging movement puzzle mini-game (Worms ninja rope mechanic)
- Museum becomes full top-down stealth puzzle with vision cones and guard manipulation
- Stats from main game translate into effects across mini-games

### 9.3 Trading / City Management Sim

- Inspired by Anno 2205 trading system
- Multiple Cat City locations producing and consuming resources
- Population happiness -> votes -> permanent mayorship
- Completing mini-games unlocks unique resources for the sim

### 9.4 Post-Mayorship — Animal Kingdom

- Scope expands from Cat City to the wider Animal Kingdom
- Managing happiness of multiple animal cities
- Trading sim scales to kingdom level
